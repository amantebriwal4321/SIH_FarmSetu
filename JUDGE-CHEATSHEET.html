Section 2
How the crash detection works

We do not predict future prices. We score crash risk from three present-tense signals.

risk = 0.45 × arrivals + 0.40 × price drop + 0.15 × season
45%
Arrivals Surge
What: How much crop is flooding the mandi vs the normal baseline (average of the first 14 days)
Math: Arrivals = 3× normal → signal = 100. Normal → signal = 0
Source: Agmarknet daily arrivals (demo: modelled from real patterns)
40%
Price Slide
What: How fast the price fell over the last 7 days
Math: Fell 30% in 7 days → signal = 100. Price rose → signal = 0
Source: Agmarknet daily prices (live for today, demo for 60-day history)
15%
Harvest Season
What: Is this a known glut month for this crop in this district?
Math: Harvest month → 100, otherwise → 0
Source: Documented crop calendars (real, hard-coded)
Example: Tomato in Kolar, November

Arrivals2.8× normal900.45 × 90 = 40.5
Price slide−28% in 7 days930.40 × 93 = 37.2
SeasonNovember (harvest)1000.15 × 100 = 15.0
Total risk score92.7 → HIGH
Say this: "We read three public signals and score how dangerous the situation looks right now. A doctor reads your vitals and says 'this is serious, act now.' Same idea. Transparent and checkable."
Never say "we predict future prices." Always say "we detect crash risk from today's signals."
Section 3
"Why not use ML to predict prices?"
1Trust. Farmers and officers need to understand why the alert fired. Our weights are stated and checkable. A neural network can't explain itself.
2Data. A price prediction model needs 5+ years of daily data per crop per district. That doesn't exist cleanly on the government's free API tier.
3Accountability. If our score is wrong, you can check which signal was off. With a black box, you can only say "the model was wrong."
Section 4
The full flow, end to end
1
Data loads — 60 days of price + arrivals history for this crop and district
2
Predictor scores each day — three signals combined into one 0-100 risk score, building a curve over time
3
Score hits HIGH — officer sees it on the dashboard with the crash chart and surplus tonnes at risk
4
Officer clicks "Route surplus" — matching engine finds nearby SHG/FPO units by distance and capacity
5
Farmer gets a phone call — in their language (English, Hindi, or Kannada), works on any phone including a keypad
6
Farmer presses 1 to accept — confirmation goes back to the officer console and the unit is notified
7
FPO van collects from villages — farmer travels zero or minimal distance
8
Unit makes paste, pays ₹9/kg — crop saved, farmer paid fairly, unit gets raw material
Section 5
"Why ₹9 when the mandi price is ₹3?"
₹3 is distress, not real supply. In a glut, most crop rots unsold. Nobody is actually buying at ₹3. A processing unit can't build a paste line on collapsing spot lots.
The unit doesn't sell it fresh. Tomato becomes paste worth ₹48/kg that lasts months. ₹9 for raw crop is cheap for them.
Both sides win. Farmer gets 2.4× the crashing mandi price plus a guaranteed buyer. Unit gets steady, fresh, traceable supply for their factory.
Section 6
"How do farmers register without being technical?"
1
FPO paper register — every FPO legally maintains a member list (name, phone, crops, land). Government requirement under the 10,000 FPO scheme.
2
Kisan Mitra digitizes it — government-employed village workers enter the register into the app on their phone. The farmer just confirms verbally.
3
Seasonal crop update — FPO coordinators already ask "what are you planting?" before sowing. We capture that answer digitally.

Result: Alerts are crop-specific. Tomato crash → only tomato farmers in that area get the call.

Say this: "We didn't invent a new process. We digitized the paper register that already exists. The farmer never touches a screen."
